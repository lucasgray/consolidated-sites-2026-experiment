Design notes
① Place destination
Fixed crosshairs, pannable map — same gesture pattern as any map app so there's no learning curve. The objective card is embedded above the sheet (not a floating overlay) so it doesn't compete with the crosshairs. "Apartment B · Maple & 4th" is a generated label from the player's real map data — it gives the story a local, personal address. The dashed out-and-back line from player → destination → player communicates the run shape without any text explanation. Open question: does the game suggest a place type ("look for an apartment building") or is it fully freeform?
② Loadout
The Scout/Collect toggle is replaced by a "Story Run" banner — the run type is dictated by the narrative, not the player's choice. A "change" link lets them re-place the destination without losing their companion choice. The destination pin is visible on the map here so the player can see where they're going before they commit. The dashed round-trip line from player to pin and back sets spatial expectations for the run.
③ During run
Two persistent run-state elements are added vs. a free run: the objective progress card (purple, shows distance remaining to Apartment B) and the out-and-back progress track (Start → Apt B → Return). These stay quiet and small so they don't crowd the map. Once the player reaches the destination and turns around, the middle node fills in and the return leg activates. Pickup collection still works exactly the same as a free run.
④ Post-run
Same structure as a free run. The story unlock card at the bottom is the emotional hook into the next session — it teases what was found at the apartment without spoiling it. Intel is shown separately (purple icon) to reinforce it's a different category from loot.
⑤ Post-run · Travel variant
Three structural changes from the standard post-run: (1) The header reflects the journey, not a workout — "4.2 mi covered" not "Nice work." (2) Journey progress replaces the story unlock position — the progress bar is the emotional payoff here, watching The Hollows get closer. (3) The encounter log is the new centrepiece — each encounter has a location stamp (mile marker + line), an outcome, and optionally a discovery. The subway car encounter shows the find-in-the-alcove pattern: the threat becomes a reward. The tunnel habitation encounter is purely narrative, no item, building the world. Pickups collected en route still appear below, same as always.
